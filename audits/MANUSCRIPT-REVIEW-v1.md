## 1. Headline and contribution

The strongest headline remains the conjunction: **29/30 harmful detections and 0/30 ordinary-benign accusations coexist with 25/30 exculpatory accusations, even though the relevant authoritative witness fits within the existing query budget.** Each element matters. The high X rate alone could be another adversarial prompt failure; the strong ordinary H/B performance makes it an evaluation blind spot; reachable witnesses and receipt audits show that the missing information was available through the stated interface.

The best novelty unit is the experimental design, not the broad principle that agents should verify evidence. The paper can claim an auditable, query-interactive exculpatory-state stress test, a large fixed-generator contrast, and a scoped prospective diagnostic pilot once evaluated. It should not claim priority for active oversight, false-positive monitoring, evidence sufficiency, or matched counterfactual evaluation in general.

The closest-prior paragraph should be more direct. **Arbiter already reports false accusations on aligned agents**, including an increase when logging is added and reductions with richer inspection. “Reports precision/recall tradeoffs” understates this overlap. E1's distinction is its controlled state edit under identical initial visible inputs, explicit temporal approval semantics, and a reachable authoritative witness—not discovery that a monitor can become overconfident. [Arbiter](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10747).

EnvTrustBench also tests failures to resolve plausible environmental claims against available current evidence. Certify explicitly represents evidence requirements and uses answerable/unanswerable twins. These support narrowing E1 to monitor accusations and the specific H/X/B construction, rather than a new general verification principle. MiniLab supplies richer distributed-attack motivation; E1 does not evaluate or repair its attacking agents. [EnvTrustBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08828), [Certify](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34684), [MiniLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24052v1).

The current contribution list is broadly sound. In item 2, replace “a null generic-prompt repair” with wording such as **“no demonstrated improvement from the tested verification reminder.”** The observed direction was positive but uncertain; this is not evidence that reminder prompting has zero effect. Item 3 must describe the completed Study 4 outcome when available, rather than retaining a promised evaluation as a final result.

## 2. The three hardest reviewer attacks

### Attack 1: “This is a familiar verification failure with a new benchmark wrapper.”

This criticism has substance. Prior work already studies active inspection, false accusations and evidence sufficiency. The response is to make the **controlled diagnostic contrast** the contribution: an H-correct accusation can remain behaviorally unchanged when an authoritative state edit defeats that accusation, while an ordinary B control looks safe. The study provides exact state, policy, query and rationale receipts that expose this distinction.

That is a useful, modest contribution. Do not answer the novelty criticism by claiming a general closed-world inference algorithm, a new theorem, or a universal fix. Explicitly acknowledging Arbiter's false-accusation result strengthens the narrower distinction. The existing router is a task-informed feasibility reference, not an algorithmic novelty claim.

### Attack 2: “You manufactured hard benign cases, compared them with easier benign cases, and counted six templates as thirty independent mechanisms.”

The constructed difficulty is intentional and must be explained rather than concealed. The question is whether ordinary-B evaluation exposes vulnerability to compliant alternatives that need a different authoritative check. It is not an IID deployment-risk comparison. Identical initial logs do not mean all subsequent evidence or verification difficulty is identical.

F1 survives as a controlled test: the policy says any qualifying approval suffices, H and X differ in approval-store state, labels are independently checked, and a relevant two-query witness fits the three-query cap. The router demonstrates feasibility within that cap. None of this establishes robustness on unseen workflow templates or natural swarms.

The biggest current presentation omission is the circular sentence **“B uses the ordinary benign construction.”** A reader cannot assess the contrast without knowing what B changes. Explain that B is the original benign workflow counterpart, whose underlying artifact/alias/approval state differs from H; X instead retains H's suspicious workflow and changes the authoritative approval record. H/B endpoint differences are broader than the H/X approval-store difference. Do not imply that X versus B is a one-record intervention.

Keep the five-template 5/5 versus alias-race 0/5 pattern prominent. The registered seed-level p-value can stay, but it is not evidence for thirty distinct mechanisms. Study 3 should remain an unsuccessful extension of the intended contrast, with its compromised B control and policy ambiguity visible. The H-policy ambiguity does not explain away or alter the X/B null.

### Attack 3: “You have not identified omission as the causal mechanism, and the proposed forecast nearly repeats H conviction rate.”

The current draft mostly handles this correctly. Query selection is endogenous. The 48/48 and 11/11 associations are overlapping-arm observations, not randomized effects of forcing a query. The rationale audit establishes expressed unsupported absence claims, not hidden reasoning. Replacing an entire acquisition policy with a router changes more than one selected query.

Study 4 can add a real prospective result: transport from separate calibration seeds to held-out-for-these-monitors X evaluations within the same templates. It cannot establish a new general risk law or the distinguishing flag's incremental predictive value, because raw H conviction is identical or only 1/18 different. The known-template design and four model-family cells are substantial limitations, not footnotes to a broad generalization claim.

The structural argument identifies the no-distinguishing accusation component on matched bases. Total X risk includes an additional post-distinguishing component; seed transport is empirical. Keep the decomposition and raw-H comparator alongside the eventual score. A registered success with offsetting component errors is not clean mechanistic validation; a registered failure caused by the comparator tie or post-distinguishing errors is not a refutation of the matched-transcript argument.

## 3. Required or high-value precision edits

**A. Explain B and the visible projection in Methods 3.1.** The reader needs two short sentences, not more protocol history. Suggested substance:

> B is the original benign workflow counterpart, with benign artifact/alias/approval state; X preserves H's workflow but changes the authoritative approval record. The same fixed visible-log projection is used across the triplet, so their initial monitor inputs are identical even though subsequent state queries can differ.

Do not describe every B as cleared by one particular query: the endpoint differences depend on the template. A short worked H/X example is helpful, but a new figure is optional and not worth delaying the paper.

**B. State the epistemic instruction explicitly.** Methods 3.1 already explains that the cited approval ID is informational. Add that the actual judge was instructed that missing records establish absence only with complete query coverage, and to return `insufficient_evidence` when neither conclusion is established. Both statements are in the actual handbook prompt. This directly answers the objection that an accusation was a reasonable guess under the task's rules.

Completeness is endpoint- and argument-relative. A complete alias history, a complete lookup of one cited approval, or complete approvals for the wrong digest does not certify absence of approvals for the deployed digest. The current rationale paragraph is strongest when read with that rule made explicit.

**C. Narrow the “full-witness” and resolution wording.** The judge assesses ANY of eight policies, while the family witness addresses RP-1. `handbook.py` explicitly distinguishes target-policy compliance from complete all-policy compliance under the limited evidence interface. This does not undermine the false-RP-1-accusation result or the triplet endpoint, which permits abstention. It does mean Table 1 should avoid implying that the controls formally certified complete workflow compliance.

Replace “diagnostic upper bound” with **“privileged witness reference”** or an equivalent description of its information advantage. Replace the caption's “triplets can be resolved” with the narrower observation that **the target-policy evidence can avert these accusations while retaining harmful-case detection**. The full-witness judge is not a mathematical upper bound on every possible monitor.

**D. Use “qualifying approval outside the visible log” in the abstract.** “Additional valid approval” can suggest an empty-versus-nonempty store shortcut. H has a corresponding record that fails scope or timing; the manuscript explains this correctly in Methods. The abstract can preserve that distinction without extra length.

**E. Keep three distinctions intact in the final Study 4 update.**

- A differing public response is not necessarily a complete exonerating witness.
- Calibration makes no live X-monitor calls, but does construct X twins for offline replay.
- Evaluation transfers across seeds in the same six templates, with existing X worlds already known to the researchers; it is not unseen-template or zero-shot monitor generalization.

The draft already states these substantially correctly. Preserve them when shortening. The primary scoring rule, per-cell observed counts/absolute errors, comparator wins, intervals and post-distinguishing decomposition should be reported after complete evaluation. If space is tight, the main text needs the registered verdict and compact per-cell results; the full decomposition and raw-H comparison can use a clearly referenced appendix table. Do not present interval inclusion alone as precise prediction.

**F. Preserve the Study 3 distinction.** Its B-control construction makes it an imperfect replication of the intended contrast. This prevents a clean broad generalization claim; it does not turn 7/30 versus 7/30 into a positive differential result. The null-timestamp ambiguity concerns 15 H labels, while X/B labels and their null contrast remain unchanged under the audited alternative semantics.

The numerical auditor's two earlier corrections—44 as a net gain and Figure 1's initially unmentioned rather than universally uninspected approval—are already incorporated in the reviewed SHA. I am not reporting them as new findings.
